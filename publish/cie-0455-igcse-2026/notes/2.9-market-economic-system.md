**What this topic asks of you**

- Define the market economic system and identify its features: who owns resources, who takes production decisions, and what role prices play in allocating those resources.
- Explain the advantages and disadvantages of the market economic system, with examples from different countries, and weigh the two sides for named groups.

---

## What a market economic system is

### The definition

A market economic system is an economic system in which resources are allocated through the price mechanism: most resources are privately owned, households and firms take the production decisions, and prices formed by demand and supply determine what is produced, how it is produced and for whom. No central authority draws up a plan of what the country should make. Instead, the answers to the three questions every economy faces - what, how and for whom to produce - emerge from the buying and selling of millions of households and firms. In Namibia, the fruit traded at the open market in Oshakati illustrates the idea: no official instructs anyone to bring mangoes, yet sellers bring them, buyers pay for them, and the price settles where the quantity sellers bring meets the quantity buyers want.

### The features to name in an answer

An answer that identifies the features of a market system names four things. Private ownership: most resources - land, shops, machines - belong to private people and firms, not to the state. Decentralised decisions: each household decides what to buy and each firm decides what to produce, so choices are made by the people living with them rather than by a planning office. The profit motive: firms produce because they expect to earn profit, which is the reward that keeps them responding to what buyers want. Freely moving prices: prices rise and fall with demand and supply, and it is these price movements that tell resources where to go. Germany and the Netherlands operate largely this way in their retail sectors: a shop owner in Hamburg decides stock, sets prices to clear it, and answers to no ministry for the choice.

## Advantages and disadvantages of the market system

### How the machinery produces its advantages

Prices act as signals and incentives. When mangoes at the Oshakati open market are scarce after a poor harvest, the price rises; the higher price tells buyers to take less and tells any farmer with fruit that bringing it is now worth the trip, so resources move to the good people want most. The same chain drives consumer sovereignty: Kaoko Juice earns revenue where buyers spend, so it tends to produce the flavours buyers choose. Competition adds a third advantage: a seller charging far above its rivals tends to lose sales, so rivalry holds prices down and choice wide. Together the profit motive and prices move resources to their most valued uses without a central plan. Vietnam's farm markets after its 1980s reforms show the same chains at work.

### Where the machinery misfires

The market system also misfires. Income is distributed unevenly, because rewards follow scarce skills and ownership: a household owning two taxis earns far more than a security guard. Street lighting in Katutura tends to be under-provided, because a firm cannot charge each person who benefits from the glow. Goods that impose costs on others may still be produced where the profit is good: a plant releasing waste into a river keeps the sales while people downstream carry the harm. And work is not guaranteed, because firms hire for expected profit, not for the need for jobs.

### Weighing the two sides

The judgement depends on the good and the group. For consumer goods - juice, clothing, haircuts - the market's advantages dominate: choice is wide, rivalry holds prices down, resources follow what buyers value. For goods that spread their benefits beyond the buyer, or for households with no resources to sell, the disadvantages bite hardest. The weighing ends with a condition: the market serves consumers well where goods can be sold one-by-one and rivalry is real. Topic 2.11 shows the compromise most countries adopt.
