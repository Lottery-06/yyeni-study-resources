## Money: forms, functions and characteristics

### What money is

Money is anything that people generally accept as payment for goods and services and for settling debts. What makes a thing money is the acceptance it carries, not its material, and money today appears as coins, notes and bank deposits moved by card or phone. A trader at the Katutura open market takes a N$20 note for tomatoes as readily as a card payment: both are money, because both are accepted. In Germany the euro plays the same role in a Munich bakery that the Namibian dollar plays in Windhoek.

### What money does

The functions of money are the jobs it does in exchange. As a medium of exchange it passes from buyer to seller: a wood-carver in Katutura sells a carving for N$80 and buys a bag of maize meal for N$45 the same afternoon, where barter would need one trader who both wants a carving and sells maize - a double coincidence of wants. As a unit of account it measures value, so Etosha Gate Lodge can quote a night at N$1 200 and compare it with the cost of providing it. As a store of value it keeps worth from one week to the next, so a worker paid on Friday can spend part of it on Sunday.

### What money needs to be

The characteristics of money are the qualities a thing needs before people accept it as money. Acceptability: sellers take it in payment. Durability: it survives being passed around. Portability: a N$100 note carries its buying power in a pocket, which a goat cannot. Divisibility: it splits into small units for small purchases. Uniformity: one N$50 note matches another, so a buyer need not inspect it. Scarcity: it is limited in supply, so it tends to hold its value.

### Two lists, not one

The register of common errors records learners mixing the characteristics of money with the functions of money; they are different lists. The functions are what money does in an exchange: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value. The characteristics are what money has to be to do those jobs: acceptable, durable, portable, divisible, uniform, scarce. The test is to ask 'is it' or 'does it': 'it passes from buyer to seller' names a function, while 'it survives being passed around' names a characteristic. A learner who answers a characteristics question with 'medium of exchange' has quoted the wrong list.

## Banking: central banks and commercial banks

### Commercial banks

A commercial bank is a financial institution that accepts deposits from households and firms, lends the pooled funds to borrowers, and moves payments between accounts. Namib Signs of Walvis Bay keeps its takings in a current account, pays its three suppliers by transfer and borrows N$250 000 for a new printing machine; around the account stand the services a commercial bank provides - safekeeping for deposits, loans, and payments moved at a distance. A family firm in Rotterdam uses its bank in the same three ways, which shows the role travels beyond Namibia.

### The central bank

A central bank is the institution at the centre of a country's banking system; in Namibia that is the Bank of Namibia, and the euro area's equivalent sits in Frankfurt, Germany. A central bank issues the notes and coins, holds the government's account and settles its payments, keeps the deposits of the commercial banks, and stands behind them as lender of last resort when deposits are withdrawn in a rush.

### Two banks, two customers

The boundary between them is who they serve. A commercial bank serves households and firms: it takes their deposits and makes them loans. The central bank serves the government and the commercial banks themselves, and does not open accounts for private customers - a worker in Oshakati banks with a commercial bank, while the government banks with the Bank of Namibia.

### Why each matters, and to whom

For producers: banks pool many small deposits into larger loans, so Kaoko Juice of Oshakati can borrow N$400 000 for bottling equipment that no single saver could finance, and bridge the weeks between paying for fruit and receiving juice revenue. For consumers: deposits are safer than notes kept at home and payments travel to a school in Gobabis without cash moving. For government: taxes and civil-service salaries move through accounts at the central bank and the commercial banks, so collections and payments have a channel. For the economy: the payments linking buyers and sellers run on this system.
