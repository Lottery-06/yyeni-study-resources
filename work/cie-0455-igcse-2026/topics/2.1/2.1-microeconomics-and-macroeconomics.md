# 2.1 Microeconomics and macroeconomics

**What this topic asks of you**

- Define microeconomics and recognise the kinds of question it asks: the choices of individual households, firms and particular markets.
- Define macroeconomics, name its economy-wide measures, and tell a macroeconomic question from a microeconomic one.

---

## Microeconomics: the study of individual choices

### The small pieces, one at a time

Microeconomics is the study of economic choices at the level of the individual unit: one household, one firm, one market. It asks how a household in Katutura divides a N$3 200 monthly income between rent, food and transport, how Otjiwa Dairy sets the price of a litre of milk, and how many guides Etosha Gate Lodge employs in peak season. The thread running through these questions is that each concerns a single decision-maker or a single market: the price of one good, the output of one firm, the jobs of one workplace. A coffee farmer in Vietnam deciding whether to plant two more hectares faces the same kind of question: her farm, her budget, her market. Microeconomics looks at the small pieces of the economy one piece at a time.

### One firm's price, one firm's question

Kaoko Juice bottles 4 000 bottles a month in Opuwo and sells each at N$12. It weighs raising the price to N$14, expecting to sell 3 600 bottles a month at the higher price. This is a microeconomic decision because it belongs to one firm selling in one market: the objects of study are the firm's price, the firm's quantity and the firm's 9 workers. A macroeconomist leaves this bottle aside and studies the economy as a whole instead: the general level of prices across the country, the total output of goods and services, and total employment. Same economy, different lens: the close-up on one unit versus the wide view of the totals.

### Connections

The demand and supply analysis of topic 2.2 is microeconomics at work, because it prices a single market; the cost analysis of topic 3.7 stays micro too, following one firm's books.

## Macroeconomics: the economy as a whole

### The wide view: totals and averages

Macroeconomics is the study of the economy as a whole. Instead of the price of one litre of milk, it examines the general level of prices across the country; instead of the output of one cannery, the total output of goods and services; instead of one firm's jobs, total employment and the share of the workforce without jobs. Its questions are the ones a finance ministry faces each year: how fast total output grew, at what rate consumer prices are rising, how many of those seeking work found none. Japan's long struggle to lift prices in the 2010s and Brazil's battle with rapid price rises in the 1990s were macroeconomic stories, reported as national conditions rather than the fortunes of any single firm.

### One average built from thousands of prices

Suppose consumer prices across Namibia rose by 4.2% in one year and by 6.1% the next. Each figure is an average across thousands of markets, and that averaging is what makes the question macroeconomic: no single carton of pilchards at N$45 explains it, and no single firm can fix it. The policy responses act on the economy as a whole: the central bank's interest-rate decision and the finance ministry's budget steer totals, not one market's price. Yet the 6.1% was built from thousands of small pricing decisions like one dairy raising the price of a litre from N$12 to N$14, which is the bridge between the two halves of the subject: the wide view is assembled from the close-ups.

### Connections

The government-policy topics of Unit 4, such as fiscal policy in topic 4.3, are macroeconomics in action, because their tools act on totals: spending, taxation and borrowing across the economy.
