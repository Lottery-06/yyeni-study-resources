## Content units

**CU-8223-2.7.1** — sub-topic 2.7.1, 13 objectives, 3530–5070 words
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-01` tier 1 — state that the mammalian circulatory system is a closed double circulation consisting of a heart, blood vessels and blood
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-02` tier 2 — observe and make plan diagrams of the structure of arteries and veins, using prepared microscope slides and be able to recognise these vessels using the light microscope or from photomicrographs
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-03` tier 2 — explain the relationship between the structure and function of arteries, veins and capillaries
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-04` tier 2 — recognise and draw the structure of red blood cells, monocytes, neutrophils and lymphocytes using prepared slides, photomicrographs and electron micrographs
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-05` tier 1 — state the functions of tissue fluid and describe the formation of tissue fluid in a capillary network
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-06` tier 2 — state and explain the differences between blood, tissue fluid and lymph
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-07` tier 3 — describe the role of red blood cells in carrying oxygen and carbon dioxide with reference to the role of: - haemoglobin - carbonic anhydrase - the formation of haemoglobinic acid - the formation of carbamino-haemoglobin (details of the chloride shift are not required)
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-08` tier 2 — describe how carbon monoxide binds with haemoglobin to form carboxyhaemoglob in reducing the affinity of haemoglobin for oxygen
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-09` tier 2 — describe the role of plasma in the transport of carbon dioxide
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-10` tier 2 — describe and explain the oxygen dissociation curve of adult haemoglobin
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-11` tier 2 — explain the importance of the oxygen dissociation curve at partial pressures of oxygen in the lungs and in respiring tissues
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-12` tier 2 — describe the Bohr shift and explain the importance of the Bohr shift
- `OBJ-8223-2.7.1-13` tier 2 — describe and explain the significance of the increase in the red blood cell count of humans at high altitude
